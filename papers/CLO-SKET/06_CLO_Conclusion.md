# 6. Conclusion

This study investigated whether garment sketches contain a
quantitatively discoverable organization of morphology that could
provide a foundation for understanding sketches as a structured
visual language.

Using 2,300 Clo-Sket garment sketches, we constructed a
reproducible 135-dimensional morphology representation from image
geometry alone. The resulting morphology representation exhibited
strong spectral concentration and formed a connected, locally
traversable geometry in which morphology variation was continuous
rather than organized as isolated observations.

Within this geometry, multiscale density analysis revealed recurring
regions of elevated morphology density. These regions were not
strongly compact or cleanly separated clusters. Instead, they
remained embedded within the broader continuous morphology space
while exhibiting reproducible quantitative morphology profiles
across observational scales.

Feature-level analyses further demonstrated that the observed
geometry is distributed across multiple complementary morphology
feature families. Global geometric descriptors, horizontal
occupancy, and vertical occupancy each contributed structural
information, while perturbation analyses showed that removing
complete feature families substantially altered the recovered local
geometry.

Importantly, quantitative properties associated with continuous
morphology variation overlapped with those distinguishing recurring
density regions. This convergence provides evidence that the
regional organization is related to the same measurable morphology
variation that structures the broader geometry.

Taken together, the findings support the following empirical
characterization:

    garment sketches occupy a continuous and connected
    quantitative morphology space containing recurring
    density-organized regions with reproducible quantitative
    profiles.

This result provides a geometric foundation for investigating the
hypothesis that garment sketches possess an underlying morphology
language.

However, the present study does not establish that the observed
regions are semantic categories, that individual quantitative
features are semantic primitives, or that a compositional morphology
grammar has been recovered. These questions require independent
semantic and perceptual validation.

The contribution of this study is therefore not the assertion that
the language of garment sketches has already been decoded. Rather,
it demonstrates that the morphology of garment sketches contains
measurable, structured, continuous, and recurring organization from
which such a language can be empirically investigated.

In this sense, the study establishes the geometric substrate for a
computational morphology language and defines the next scientific
problem:

    quantitative morphology
            ↓
    structured morphology geometry
            ↓
    recurring morphology organization
            ↓
    semantic grounding
            ↓
    morphology primitives
            ↓
    compositional morphology grammar

The first three levels are supported by the present evidence.
The remaining levels constitute the next stage of the research.
