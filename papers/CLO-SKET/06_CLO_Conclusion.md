# 6. Conclusion

This study investigated whether garment sketches contain a
quantitatively discoverable organization of morphology that could
provide a foundation for understanding sketches as structured visual
objects and, ultimately, for investigating a computational morphology
language.

Using 2,300 Clo-Sket garment sketches, we constructed a reproducible
135-dimensional morphology representation from image geometry alone.
After source-only standardization, the representation exhibited
concentrated spectral structure and formed a connected, locally
traversable morphology geometry. The observed relationships indicate
that morphology variation is structured within a continuous-valued
quantitative representation rather than behaving as an arbitrary
collection of independent observations.

Within this geometry, multiscale density analysis revealed recurring
regions of elevated morphology density. These regions were not
strongly compact or cleanly separated clusters. Instead, they
remained embedded within the broader morphology geometry while
exhibiting measurable quantitative morphology differences and
reproducible regional feature profiles across observational scales.

Permutation analyses strengthened this interpretation. Same-region
nearest-neighbor retention exceeded a size-preserving random
assignment null at every density scale. Cross-scale regional
feature-discrimination profiles also showed substantially greater
agreement than expected under independently permuted,
size-preserving region assignments. These results support a
non-random relationship between density-defined regional
organization and quantitative morphology, together with
cross-scale reproducibility of regional morphology profiles.

Feature-level analyses further demonstrated that the observed
morphology geometry is distributed across multiple complementary
feature families. Global geometric descriptors, horizontal
occupancy, and vertical occupancy each contributed measurable
structural information. Perturbation analyses showed that removing
complete feature families altered the recovered local geometry,
while ordered occupancy profiles exhibited reproducible spatial
locality relative to a coordinate-order permutation null.

Importantly, these findings remain quantitative. The recurring
density regions are not interpreted as morphology categories, and
the discriminating morphology features are not interpreted as
semantic primitives. Likewise, the observed connected geometry does
not establish a mathematical manifold, and the observed local
structure does not establish a formal morphology grammar or
continuous semantic dimensions.

Taken together, the findings support the following empirical
characterization:

> **Garment sketches exhibit structured quantitative morphology
> organization within a connected morphology geometry, including
> recurring density-organized regions with reproducible quantitative
> morphology profiles across observational scales.**

This result provides a quantitative geometric foundation for
investigating whether the organization of garment sketches can
subsequently be grounded in semantic or perceptual structure.

However, the present study does not establish:

- discrete morphology categories;
- semantic morphology primitives;
- named garment concepts;
- a compositional morphology grammar;
- a mathematical morphology manifold;
- causal morphology variables; or
- an exact intrinsic dimensionality of garment morphology.

These questions require independent empirical investigation,
including semantic and perceptual validation.

The contribution of this study is therefore not the assertion that
the language of garment sketches has already been decoded. Rather,
it demonstrates that garment-sketch morphology contains measurable,
structured, locally organized, connected, and recurring quantitative
organization from which such a language can be empirically
investigated.

The resulting scientific progression is:

    quantitative morphology
            ↓
    structured quantitative geometry
            ↓
    recurring quantitative organization
            ↓
    independent perceptual validation
            ↓
    semantic morphology interpretation
            ↓
    validated morphology primitives
            ↓
    possible compositional morphology grammar

The present study establishes the first three quantitative levels.
The remaining levels are intentionally left as open empirical
questions for subsequent research.
