# 5. Discussion

## 5.1 From Feature Representation to Morphology Geometry

The central finding of this study is that quantitative
representation of Clo-Sket garment sketches reveals a structured
morphology geometry rather than an unorganized collection of
independent measurements.

The 135-dimensional representation was constructed directly from
image geometry and contained both spatial occupancy measurements and
global geometric descriptors. After source-only standardization,
the morphology variance was strongly concentrated in a substantially
smaller set of principal directions. This concentration indicates
that the measured morphology properties are not varying independently
across the full feature space.

More importantly, the dimensionality reduction was followed by
evidence of local continuity and global connectivity. Nearby
observations occupied progressively related neighborhoods, while the
10-nearest-neighbor morphology graph connected all 2,300 sketches
within a single component. Successive local transitions also
remained relatively close to the characteristic local morphology
scale.

Together, these findings suggest that the feature representation
captures relationships among sketches rather than merely assigning
independent numerical descriptors to them. In other words, the
representation defines a geometry in which differences between
garment sketches can be examined as structured quantitative
variation.

This distinction is important for the broader hypothesis of a
computational morphology language. A language-like representation
requires more than a list of measurable attributes; it requires
relationships among those attributes and among the observations
described by them. The present results provide evidence for such
relational organization at the geometric level.

However, the geometry should not be interpreted as a symbolic
language at this stage. The observed continuity does not identify
semantic units, and the absence of a globally directed trajectory
does not support an ordered sequence of morphology states. Likewise,
the 73-dimensional PCA representation and the approximately
10.55-dimensional participation-ratio effective dimensionality
describe variance structure rather than establishing an exact
mathematical manifold.

The more defensible interpretation is therefore that garment
sketches occupy a structured quantitative morphology space in which
morphological variation is relational, locally continuous, and
globally connected.

This provides the geometric foundation for the subsequent finding
that recurring density organization and quantitative regional
profiles emerge within the same space. The regional structures are
therefore interpreted in the context of an underlying continuous
morphology geometry rather than as independently imposed
categories.

The distinction between quantitative geometry and semantic
interpretation is central to the present study. The results establish
the former; whether particular regions or measurable properties
correspond to semantic morphology units remains an empirical
question requiring additional evidence.

## 5.2 Continuous Organization Rather Than Discrete Categories

The morphology geometry identified in this study is better
characterized as **continuous organization containing recurring
regional structure** than as a collection of discrete morphology
categories.

Several observations support this interpretation. First, local
morphology transitions were relatively smooth: successive
nearest-neighbor transitions remained close to the characteristic
one-step distance. Second, the morphology graph was globally
connected, with all 2,300 observations belonging to a single
connected component. These findings indicate that the recurring
regions identified later in the analysis are embedded within a
larger connected morphology space rather than separated by
systematic gaps.

The multiscale density analysis nevertheless revealed recurring
regions of elevated density. Importantly, these regions did not
behave like conventional compact clusters. Within-region distances
were not consistently smaller than between-region distances, while
local same-region neighbor retention was substantial. Regional
feature profiles also exhibited negative silhouette values,
indicating considerable overlap between regional profiles.

This combination of findings is important. If the morphology
organization were fundamentally categorical, one would expect the
identified regions to exhibit stronger global separation and
relatively compact internal structure. Instead, the evidence shows
that observations can occupy recurring regions while retaining
continuous variation within and between those regions.

The resulting structure can therefore be conceptualized as:

    continuous morphology space
             ↓
    regions of elevated density
             ↓
    overlapping quantitative profiles
             ↓
    continuous variation within and between regions

The density regions consequently appear better understood as
**recurring modes of quantitative morphology organization** than as
discrete morphology classes.

This interpretation also explains why density organization remained
reproducible across observational scales without producing an
invariant partition. Changes in scale altered basin number and
membership, while quantitative regional profile structure remained
highly reproducible. The morphology organization therefore appears
to possess structure at multiple observational scales rather than a
single canonical categorical partition.

For the proposed morphology-language framework, this distinction is
particularly consequential. A language-like organization need not
require every morphology state to form a mutually exclusive category.
The present evidence instead suggests that morphology properties may
combine continuously, producing regions of increased observational
density within a broader space of possible configurations.

Such an organization is compatible with the idea that garment
sketches encode recurring morphological configurations without
requiring those configurations to function as discrete symbolic
classes.

However, this interpretation remains geometric rather than
semantic. The recurring regions cannot yet be called morphological
"words," "concepts," or "primitives" solely from their density
structure. Establishing such a correspondence would require
independent evidence that particular quantitative configurations
consistently map onto meaningful morphological units.

The principal contribution of the present result is therefore the
identification of a middle ground between two overly simple
descriptions:

    independent continuous measurements
                  versus
    discrete morphology categories

The Clo-Sket evidence instead supports:

    **structured continuous morphology with recurring
    regional organization.**

This provides a more appropriate geometric foundation for testing
whether a semantic language of garment sketches can subsequently be
identified.

## 5.3 Why Recurring Density Regions Matter

The identification of recurring density organization within a
continuous morphology space provides an important intermediate level
of structure between individual measurements and discrete semantic
categories.

A purely feature-level description would treat each garment sketch
as a point defined by a collection of quantitative measurements. The
geometry results show that these measurements are not independent:
sketches occupy a connected morphology space in which some
quantitative configurations occur more frequently than others.

The multiscale density analysis provides evidence for this recurring
organization. Across independently derived observational scales,
density landscapes repeatedly produced regions of elevated
observational density. Although the number and membership of
individual basins changed with scale, the quantitative morphology
profiles associated with these regions remained highly reproducible.

This distinction between basin identity and profile persistence is
important. A density basin is not treated as a fixed entity that must
retain the same membership at every scale. Instead, the persistence
of quantitative profile structure indicates that similar forms of
morphological organization recur even when the precise boundaries of
the regions change.

Such scale-dependent recurrence is more consistent with a structured
continuous morphology space than with a single categorical
partition. In a categorical representation, changing the
observational scale would ideally preserve a fixed set of mutually
exclusive classes. In the present data, the observed organization is
more flexible: regions can expand, contract, merge, or reorganize
while retaining recognizable quantitative morphology profiles.

The importance of these regions therefore lies not in their serving
as predefined classes, but in their revealing where quantitative
morphology configurations repeatedly concentrate within the broader
space.

This provides a useful intermediate representation:

    individual morphology measurements
                 ↓
    continuous morphology geometry
                 ↓
    recurring density regions
                 ↓
    quantitative regional profiles
                 ↓
    candidate morphological organization

The regional structures also showed a meaningful relationship to
the continuous geometry. The quantitative properties associated with
continuous morphology variation overlapped with those that
distinguished the recurring density regions. Thus, the density
organization does not appear to be an unrelated partition imposed on
the feature space; it emerges from and reflects variation already
present in the morphology geometry.

At the same time, the absence of strong global compactness and the
negative silhouette values are informative. They indicate that the
recurring regions cannot simply be interpreted as well-separated
morphology classes. Their significance instead arises from the
combination of local coherence, reproducibility, scale persistence,
and quantitative profile differences.

This provides a potentially useful framework for understanding
garment morphology as a structured system of recurring configurations
embedded within continuous variation. Such a framework is compatible
with a language-like interpretation because meaningful units in a
representational system need not correspond to perfectly separated
clusters.

However, the present evidence establishes only the geometric and
quantitative level of this organization. Whether recurring density
regions correspond to perceptually meaningful garment concepts, to
recognizable design units, or to compositional morphology primitives
requires independent semantic validation.

The significance of the present finding is therefore not that a
fixed vocabulary of morphology states has been discovered. Rather,
the results identify a reproducible quantitative organization from
which such a vocabulary could potentially be investigated.

## 5.4 From Recurring Quantitative Structure Toward a Morphology Language

The results provide a geometric basis for considering garment
sketches as a structured representational system rather than as
images containing only independent visual measurements.

A language-like interpretation requires some form of organization
through which recurring configurations can be distinguished,
related, and potentially interpreted. The present study establishes
several of these lower-level requirements quantitatively. The
morphology representation exhibits concentrated variance, connected
local geometry, smooth transitions, recurring density organization,
and reproducible quantitative regional profiles.

Importantly, these properties occur simultaneously.

The evidence therefore suggests that garment morphology can be
described at multiple organizational levels:

    pixel-level sketch
            ↓
    quantitative morphology features
            ↓
    continuous morphology geometry
            ↓
    recurring density regions
            ↓
    quantitative morphology profiles
            ↓
    candidate morphological units

This hierarchy provides a computational framework in which a
possible morphology language could be investigated without assuming
its vocabulary in advance.

The recurring regions are particularly relevant because they provide
candidate organizational units that emerge from the morphology
distribution itself. Their boundaries are not fixed across
observational scales, and their profiles remain partially
overlapping. This suggests that any eventual morphology vocabulary
may be better represented as structured regions or configurations in
a continuous space than as a collection of mutually exclusive
symbols.

The feature analyses provide a complementary perspective. Multiple
quantitative properties contribute to the continuous geometry, and
the same properties participate in regional differences. This
indicates that candidate morphology units are unlikely to be
represented adequately by single scalar measurements. Instead, they
may correspond to combinations of spatial and global geometric
properties.

This observation is important for the original hypothesis of a
semantic language of fashion sketches. If morphology organization is
distributed across combinations of measurable properties, then a
future computational language model may need to represent
relationships among morphology properties rather than simply assign
individual labels to isolated visual features.

However, the present study deliberately stops before making that
semantic claim.

The observed density regions are not yet demonstrated to correspond
to named garment concepts. The participating features are not yet
demonstrated to be semantic primitives. No grammar or compositional
rules have been inferred, and no evidence has yet established that
one morphology configuration consistently corresponds to one
semantic interpretation.

Consequently, the present results should be viewed as establishing
the **geometric substrate from which a morphology language can be
investigated**, rather than as having already recovered the language
itself.

The distinction can be summarized as:

    morphology geometry
            ↓
    recurring quantitative organization
            ↓
    candidate morphological units
            ↓
    semantic validation
            ↓
    morphology vocabulary
            ↓
    compositional morphology grammar

The first three levels are supported by the present evidence. The
remaining semantic and grammatical levels constitute the next
research problem.

## 5.5 What the Present Evidence Establishes — and What It Does Not

The findings support a specific and bounded conclusion about the
organization of garment-sketch morphology.

At the quantitative level, the evidence demonstrates that Clo-Sket
sketches occupy a structured morphology geometry rather than an
arbitrary collection of independent feature measurements. This
geometry is characterized by concentrated variance, global
connectivity, locally smooth transitions, and recurring
multiscale density organization.

At the regional level, independently discovered density regions
exhibit reproducible quantitative morphology profiles. These
profiles remain recognizable across observational scales even though
individual basin membership and basin number change with scale.

At the representation level, morphology geometry is distributed
across multiple complementary feature families. Horizontal
occupancy, vertical occupancy, and global morphology descriptors
each contribute measurable structural information, while the
perturbation analyses indicate that the geometry is not controlled
by a small number of isolated measurements.

Together, these findings support the following empirical statement:

    garment sketches exhibit structured quantitative morphology
    organization within a continuous and connected geometry,
    including recurring density-organized regions with
    reproducible quantitative profiles.

Several stronger interpretations, however, remain unsupported by the
present evidence.

First, the recurring density regions cannot yet be identified as
semantic morphology categories. Their global compactness is weak,
their profiles overlap, and their boundaries vary with observational
scale.

Second, individual quantitative features cannot yet be designated
as semantic morphology primitives. Their association with geometric
variation establishes measurable morphological relevance, but not
semantic identity.

Third, no morphology grammar has yet been demonstrated. The present
analysis does not establish compositional rules governing how
candidate morphology units combine to form complete garment
structures.

Fourth, the observed geometry does not establish a causal hierarchy
of morphology properties. Feature influence under perturbation is
evidence of structural contribution within the representation, not
causal importance in garment design.

Finally, neither the 73-dimensional PCA representation nor the
approximately 10.55-dimensional participation-ratio effective
dimensionality should be interpreted as the exact dimension of a
mathematical morphology manifold.

These boundaries are not limitations of interpretation alone; they
define the next empirical stage of the research.

The present study therefore establishes the quantitative geometric
substrate required to investigate a morphology language, while
leaving semantic grounding and compositional grammar as open
questions.

The distinction can be summarized as:

    ESTABLISHED

    quantitative morphology representation
                ↓
    structured continuous geometry
                ↓
    recurring regional organization
                ↓
    reproducible quantitative profiles


    NOT YET ESTABLISHED

    quantitative morphology property
                ↓
    semantic primitive
                ↓
    morphology vocabulary
                ↓
    compositional grammar

## 5.6 Limitations and Future Empirical Tests

The present study establishes a quantitative geometry of garment
sketch morphology, but several important questions remain unresolved.
These limitations define specific empirical directions rather than
representing failures of the present analysis.

### 5.6.1 Dataset scope

The present geometry was established using the Clo-Sket source
dataset comprising 2,300 sketches. The extent to which the observed
morphology organization generalizes to other sketch collections,
drawing styles, garment categories, designers, or annotation
protocols remains to be established.

An important next test is therefore external replication using
independently collected garment-sketch datasets. Reproduction of the
same spectral, geometric, and multiscale regional organization would
provide stronger evidence that the observed structure reflects
properties of garment morphology rather than characteristics specific
to Clo-Sket.

### 5.6.2 Representation dependence

The present findings are conditional on the canonical 135-dimensional
morphology representation defined in M02.

Although feature-block complementarity and perturbation analyses
demonstrate that the observed geometry is distributed across the
three feature families, alternative quantitative representations may
recover additional or different aspects of morphology structure.

Future work should therefore test whether the major geometric
properties identified here remain stable under independently
constructed morphology representations.

Such analyses would distinguish structure intrinsic to garment
morphology from structure introduced by a particular feature
engineering scheme.

### 5.6.3 Semantic grounding of candidate morphology units

The recurring density regions and measurable morphology properties
identified in this study are quantitative structures. Their
correspondence to human-recognizable morphology concepts has not yet
been established.

A direct next experiment is to obtain independent human judgments
about morphology similarity, region membership, or specific
morphological properties and test their correspondence with the
quantitative geometry.

Agreement between independently derived quantitative regions and
human-perceived morphology organization would provide evidence for
semantic grounding.

Conversely, systematic disagreement would identify where the
quantitative representation captures geometric structure that is not
perceptually or semantically salient.

### 5.6.4 Validation of morphology primitives

The present study identifies candidate quantitative morphology
properties but does not establish semantic morphology primitives.

A stronger primitive claim would require independent validation that
a candidate property or combination of properties corresponds
consistently to a recognizable morphological unit across sketches.

Future experiments should therefore test whether candidate
morphological properties can be identified reliably by independent
observers and whether they remain stable across different garment
contexts.

This would provide a direct empirical test of the transition:

    quantitative morphology property
                ↓
    perceptually meaningful morphology unit

rather than assuming that statistical importance implies semantic
status.

### 5.6.5 Compositionality and morphology grammar

The present geometry establishes organization among morphology
observations but does not establish rules governing the composition
of morphology units.

A morphology grammar would require evidence that combinations of
morphological properties or candidate primitives occur according to
reproducible structural relationships.

Future work should therefore test whether combinations of validated
morphology units can predict or explain larger garment structures,
and whether these combinations exhibit recurring compositional
constraints.

This represents a distinct empirical question from identifying
individual morphology primitives.

### 5.6.6 External and held-out validation

The present analyses establish the morphology geometry and regional
organization within the source dataset. Stronger evidence would be
obtained by evaluating the frozen source representation and
transformations on independently held-out sketches.

In particular, future validation should test whether:

    source morphology geometry
            ↓
    frozen representation
            ↓
    regional organization
            ↓
    quantitative morphology profiles

can be reproduced without refitting the underlying transformations
to the evaluation data.

Such validation would strengthen the claim that the observed
organization represents reproducible morphology structure rather than
source-specific geometry.

### 5.6.7 Structural influence versus causality

Feature perturbation demonstrates that different feature families
contribute to preservation of the observed morphology geometry.
However, perturbational influence within a representation should not
be interpreted as causal influence in garment construction or design.

Establishing causal morphology relationships would require
interventions defined at the level of garment structure or controlled
generation of morphology changes.

The present findings therefore remain appropriately limited to
structural contribution within the quantitative representation.

### 5.6.8 From quantitative geometry to semantic language

The central unresolved question is whether the structured
quantitative morphology space identified here can be grounded in a
semantic system.

The present evidence supports:

    quantitative morphology representation
                ↓
    structured continuous geometry
                ↓
    recurring regional organization
                ↓
    reproducible quantitative morphology profiles

The next empirical stage must test:

    quantitative morphology structure
                ↓
    perceptually validated morphology units
                ↓
    semantic morphology primitives
                ↓
    compositional relationships
                ↓
    morphology vocabulary
                ↓
    morphology grammar

This progression should be tested incrementally rather than
assuming that geometric organization is itself semantic language.

The present study therefore establishes the quantitative geometric
substrate from which such a language can be investigated. It does not
claim to have already recovered the complete semantic language of
garment sketches.

The central limitation is consequently also the central opportunity:

    **we have demonstrated structured morphology space;
    the next question is whether that structure can be
    empirically grounded as a semantic system.**
