## 2. Related Work

Computational research on fashion and sketches has developed several forms of structured visual representation. These range from garment categories and attributes in fashion-image datasets to geometric, semantic, and relational representations of sketches.

Fashion-image datasets such as DeepFashion and DeepFashion2 established structured representations of garments through categories, attributes, landmarks, masks, and related annotations. Fashionpedia extends this approach through an expert-defined ontology of apparel categories, parts, and attributes, providing detailed semantic annotations for fashion images.

Computational sketch research has explored representations based on strokes, points, geometry, and learned visual features, reflecting the importance of sketch-specific structure for recognition and interpretation. Sketch-a-Net demonstrated that sketch recognition benefits from representations designed for the distinctive visual properties of sketches, including their abstraction and deformation. Other sketch-understanding approaches have converted digital ink into symbolic representations and incorporated contextual or co-occurrence information among the resulting symbols.

Fashion-sketch research has also represented garment structure through geometric descriptors, semantic component segmentation, and component-level relational or topological representations. These representations have supported tasks such as garment interpretation, modeling, matching, and retrieval, but they differ in how the underlying components are defined and in the downstream objectives they address. These studies show that geometric, component-level, and relational representations can capture meaningful structure in fashion sketches.

Grammar-based approaches provide another way of representing structured visual organization, particularly through compositional relationships or production rules. Classical shape grammars represent design languages through predefined shapes and production rules, whereas the present study examines whether grammar-like organization can emerge from recurring relationships in observed sketch data.

Context-aware garment modeling and graph-based garment representations have further shown that relationships among geometric components can be useful for interpreting or matching garment structure. These approaches focus on interpreting, modeling, or matching established garment structures rather than deriving a reusable vocabulary from recurring sketch geometry.

Recent fashion-sketch resources have further established large-scale sketch collections for computational learning, including datasets that pair sketches with garment categories or textual descriptions. GarmentSketch, for example, provides a large-scale collection of fashion sketches paired with detailed textual descriptions for multimodal learning and sketch-to-fashion generation. GarmentSketch demonstrates the growing use of fashion sketches as computational inputs, but its primary focus is downstream multimodal learning and generation rather than the internal structural organization of sketch geometry.

Taken together, prior work provides multiple representations of fashion sketches, but the relationship between recurring geometric components and their broader structural organization remains less clearly established. This study therefore examines whether recurring sketch geometry can support a reusable component vocabulary and its broader structural characterization. The present work focuses on the internal structural organization of fashion sketches, beginning with recurring geometric components rather than predefined semantic categories.
