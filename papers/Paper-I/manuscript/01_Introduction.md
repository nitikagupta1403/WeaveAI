# 1. Introduction

Fashion sketches provide a compact representation of garment design through simplified visual marks. Their geometric and structural properties support computational sketch understanding. However, representing this structure remains a challenge because existing approaches use predefined or task-specific representations of sketch elements. These include geometric descriptors, semantic components, point and stroke structures, and topological relationships, with many approaches relying on predefined semantic categories or fixed geometric primitives. The present study therefore investigates whether recurring geometric structures in fashion sketches can be identified and organized into a reusable structural vocabulary.

The identified components are then examined for recurring sequential relationships across sketches. These relationships are used to characterize broader patterns within the sketch corpus. The geometric form of these components is then examined for variability across instances and structural contexts. These representations are then integrated to examine higher-order relationships within the learned structure. The higher-order relationships are interpreted as potential semantic organization within the learned structure. The framework therefore proceeds from observed sketch geometry to learned structure and higher-order interpretation.

The resulting representation provides a geometry-derived basis for studying the structure of fashion sketches. The representation does not assume that the resulting structure corresponds to human semantic understanding. The contribution is a geometry-derived representation of fashion-sketch structure.
