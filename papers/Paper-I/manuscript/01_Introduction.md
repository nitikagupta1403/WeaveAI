# 1. Introduction

Fashion sketches provide a compact representation of garment design through simplified visual marks. Their geometric and structural properties support computational sketch understanding. However, representing this structure remains a challenge because existing approaches use different predefined or task-specific representations of sketch elements. Existing work has represented fashion and clothing sketches using geometric descriptors, semantic components, point and stroke structures, and topological relationships. While these representations capture important aspects of sketch structure, they generally begin with predefined semantic categories, fixed geometric primitives, or task-specific representations. The question of whether a reusable structural vocabulary can be derived directly from recurring geometric organization in fashion sketches therefore remains open. This study investigates whether recurring geometric structures in fashion sketches can be identified and organized into a reusable computational vocabulary. The resulting primitives are characterized by their organization within and across sketches, including their sequential relationships and broader corpus-level patterns. Their geometric variability is characterized across observed instances and structural contexts. These representations are then integrated to examine whether higher-order relationships can be derived from the learned structure. The higher-order relationships are interpreted as potential semantic organization within the learned structure. Together, these representations provide a structured pathway from sketch geometry to higher-order interpretation.

Previous studies have established several approaches for representing and interpreting fashion and clothing sketches. These include geometric representations, semantic component segmentation, structured point and stroke representations, and component-level topology. These approaches demonstrate that geometric and structural information can support sketch understanding, but their representations are generally task-oriented or based on predefined semantic categories and annotations. The present study focuses on exploring whether a structural vocabulary of fashion sketches can be derived from recurring geometric patterns. The study therefore examines whether recurring geometric structures can provide a basis for identifying reusable components within a sketch corpus. Once identified, these components can be examined for recurring relationships within and across sketches. Their sequential relationships are examined to determine whether component identities exhibit recurring patterns. These recurring relationships are then considered as a basis for describing broader corpus-level structural patterns. The geometric form of these components may vary across their observed instances. This variability is further related to the structural contexts in which the components occur. These representations are then integrated to examine higher-order relationships within the learned structure. This provides a pathway for examining semantic organization without using predefined semantic categories to construct the initial vocabulary. The framework therefore proceeds from visual evidence to learned structure and then to higher-order interpretation.

This leads to a representation that connects geometric structure with increasingly abstract levels of description within a common framework. The framework keeps these representations connected to the underlying sketch geometry. The framework derives its representation from the observed structure of the sketch corpus rather than from a task-specific semantic representation. The framework therefore provides a geometry-derived representation for studying the structure of fashion sketches. The central question is whether recurring components of fashion sketches can be discovered directly from their geometry and organized into a reusable representation. The contribution is a geometry-derived representation of fashion-sketch structure.
