# 5. Conclusion

Fashion sketches contain recurring geometric structure that can be represented computationally as a learned vocabulary of reusable primitives. Starting from continuous sketch geometry, the framework identified 12 recurring geometry primitives across 333 garment sketches and characterized their morphology, positional organization, sequential relationships, and use within complete garment sequences.

The learned primitives showed strong morphological coherence, with substantially greater similarity among instances assigned to the same primitive than among instances assigned to different primitives. Primitive identities were also associated with characteristic positions and sequential neighborhoods within garment representations.

Sequential analysis further showed that primitive ordering was not arbitrary. Specific primitive transitions were statistically enriched or depleted relative to within-garment permutation, and immediate primitive context improved prediction of subsequent primitives, including on garments excluded from transition estimation. Together, these findings indicate that the learned geometric vocabulary contains structured sequential dependencies.

Complete garments could subsequently be represented as ordered compositions of a relatively small subset of the shared primitive vocabulary. This provides a geometry-derived intermediate representation linking local geometric structure to corpus-level sequential organization and complete garment representations.

The findings support interpreting these recurring sequential regularities as a **corpus-derived, grammar-like organization of garment geometry**. This interpretation remains deliberately narrower than a claim of a complete or universal language of fashion design. The evidence establishes computational organization within the learned representation, while human semantic validity and broader generalization remain open questions.
