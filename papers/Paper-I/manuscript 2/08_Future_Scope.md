# 6. Future Scope

Several directions follow naturally from the present representation.

## 6.1 Cross-Corpus and Cross-Designer Validation

The learned primitive vocabulary and sequential organization should be evaluated on independent fashion-sketch corpora and across sketches produced by different designers. Such validation would determine whether the identified geometric primitives and transition structures represent general properties of fashion sketches or properties specific to the present corpus and sketching conventions.

## 6.2 Expansion of Geometric Representation

The current representation is derived primarily from the vertical width signature of a sketch. Future work could incorporate additional geometric signals, including two-dimensional contour structure, local topology, intersections, symmetry, and relationships among multiple sketch components. This could reveal structural information that is not captured by a one-dimensional width representation.

## 6.3 Richer Sequential Models

The present analysis primarily evaluates first-order primitive dependencies. Future work could examine longer-range dependencies, hierarchical sequences, and higher-order transition models to determine whether garment organization contains structure extending beyond immediate primitive context.

## 6.4 Human Semantic Validation

The learned primitives and structural roles should be compared with independently obtained annotations from fashion designers or domain experts. Such evaluation would help determine whether computationally learned geometric organization corresponds to recognizable design concepts and would provide an external basis for validating higher-order semantic interpretations.

## 6.5 Context-Dependent Morphology

The immediate-context analysis did not identify a statistically significant morphological effect under the tested conditions. This leaves open the possibility that geometric realization may depend on broader or more structured contexts. Future analyses could examine longer sequential contexts, positional context, neighboring primitive combinations, or other geometric variables while maintaining appropriate controls for multiple testing.

## 6.6 Hierarchical and Compositional Representation

The observed organization suggests a potential hierarchy from geometric events to primitives, primitive families, and complete garment sequences. Future work could investigate whether such representations can be learned jointly and whether higher-level structures can be inferred directly from lower-level geometric evidence.

## 6.7 Relation to Design Processes

Finally, the learned structural representation could be compared with documented design processes and expert reasoning. Such work could clarify whether corpus-derived geometric regularities correspond to recurring construction strategies used by designers or instead represent statistical regularities specific to the visual form of completed sketches.
