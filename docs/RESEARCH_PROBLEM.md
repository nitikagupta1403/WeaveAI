# Research Problem

## Towards Computational Representation of Creative Intent in Fashion

## Abstract

Fashion is a unique creative discipline in which the final outcome often remains uncertain until late in the design and manufacturing process. Unlike many engineering disciplines, where designs can be analysed and evaluated before production using established computational models, couture and bespoke fashion continue to rely heavily on human expertise, craftsmanship, and iterative physical prototyping.

Although artificial intelligence has significantly advanced image generation, virtual try-on, and fashion visualisation, existing systems primarily operate on garments that already exist or on images that approximate their appearance. They provide limited support for reasoning about garments before they are created.

This research investigates whether a computational representation of creative intent can enable AI systems to reason about fashion during the earliest stages of the design process, thereby reducing uncertainty while preserving human creativity.

---

# Background

A fashion sketch represents only the beginning of a design journey.

The final garment emerges through a sequence of interconnected creative and technical decisions involving:

- silhouette
- garment construction
- fabric selection
- material behaviour
- pattern development
- body morphology
- tailoring techniques
- decorative components
- craftsmanship
- aesthetic judgement

Each decision influences the others. Consequently, the quality and character of the final garment cannot be determined solely from the initial sketch.

This uncertainty affects both designers and customers.

For designers, communicating a creative vision before a garment is physically constructed remains challenging. Sketches, mood boards, references, and fabric swatches each communicate part of the design, but none fully capture how these elements interact to create the final garment.

For customers, uncertainty is often even greater.

Typical questions include:

- Will this design suit me?
- Will this fabric behave the way I imagine?
- Will the final garment reflect the elegance of the original sketch?
- Is this garment worth investing in before seeing the finished result?

---

# Limitations of Existing AI Systems

Recent advances in artificial intelligence have produced impressive systems for:

- image generation
- virtual try-on
- garment visualisation
- fashion recommendation

These systems primarily optimise visual realism.

However, they generally provide limited support for reasoning about garments before they exist.

Current systems rarely answer questions such as:

- Why does this design communicate a particular aesthetic?
- How do construction decisions influence the final garment?
- How does fabric behaviour alter silhouette?
- How should a design adapt to different individuals while preserving its creative identity?

These limitations suggest that the primary challenge is not image generation alone.

Rather, fashion currently lacks a computational representation capable of preserving and reasoning about a designer's creative intent throughout the design process.

---

# Research Problem

The absence of a structured computational representation of creative intent limits the ability of current AI systems to support early-stage fashion design.

Without such a representation, intelligent systems struggle to reason about relationships between design philosophy, garment construction, materials, body morphology, aesthetics, and personalisation before a garment has been manufactured.

As a result, uncertainty remains high throughout the creative process for both designers and customers.

---

# Research Hypothesis

This research proposes that a structured representation of designer intent can enable AI systems to reason about fashion beyond image generation.

Such a representation may provide a foundation for:

- design exploration
- customer communication
- personalisation
- explainable AI-assisted design
- informed decision-making before manufacturing begins

Within the Weave AI research programme, this proposed representation is referred to as **Design DNA**.

---

# Research Scope

This research does not seek to replace fashion designers or automate the creative process.

Instead, it investigates how computational representations of creative intent can augment human creativity by reducing uncertainty, improving communication, and supporting informed design decisions throughout the earliest stages of garment creation.

---

# Research Questions

## RQ1

How can a designer's creative intent be represented computationally while preserving individuality, creativity, and aesthetic reasoning?

---

## RQ2

Can garments be described using a canonical representation that is independent of sketches, images, or finished products?

---

## RQ3

How should garment construction, material behaviour, body morphology, and personalisation be integrated into such a representation?

---

## RQ4

How can AI reason over this representation to support design exploration, customer communication, and creative decision-making before manufacturing begins?

---

## RQ5

How should systems that model creative intent be evaluated beyond image quality using measures such as explainability, consistency, usefulness, and designer satisfaction?

---

# Long-Term Vision

The long-term objective of Weave AI is to investigate computational methods that enable intelligent systems to understand, represent, and support human creativity throughout the fashion design process.

Rather than focusing solely on generating realistic images, the research aims to develop AI systems capable of reasoning about creative intent, preserving designer individuality, and assisting both designers and customers before a single piece of fabric is cut.
