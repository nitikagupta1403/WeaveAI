# Design Workflow

## Modelling the Creative Process in Fashion Design

## Purpose

The objective of this document is to describe the cognitive workflow followed by fashion designers during the creation of a garment.

Rather than viewing fashion as a sequence of isolated technical tasks, this document models design as a continuous process of observation, reasoning, experimentation, evaluation, and refinement.

Understanding this workflow is essential for developing AI systems that assist designers without replacing human creativity.

---

# Introduction

Fashion design is rarely a linear process.

Designers continuously move between inspiration, experimentation, technical constraints, aesthetic judgement, and customer requirements.

Ideas evolve through repeated refinement rather than following a fixed sequence of steps.

Consequently, an intelligent design assistant should support this iterative creative process rather than enforce a rigid workflow.

The workflow presented here represents the current research hypothesis of how creative fashion design progresses.

---

# Stage 1 — Inspiration

The creative process begins with inspiration.

Sources may include:

- Nature
- Architecture
- Art
- Music
- History
- Culture
- Travel
- Personal experiences
- Social trends
- Textures
- Colours
- Materials

### Output

A creative direction rather than a finished design.

---

# Stage 2 — Design Intent

The designer translates inspiration into intent.

Typical questions include:

- What story should this garment tell?
- Who is the intended wearer?
- What emotions should it evoke?
- What problem does it solve?
- What values should it communicate?

### Output

A clearly defined creative objective.

---

# Stage 3 — Concept Exploration

Multiple ideas are explored.

Activities may include:

- Mood boards
- Thumbnail sketches
- Colour exploration
- Silhouette exploration
- Material exploration
- Reference collection

### Output

Several candidate concepts.

---

# Stage 4 — Concept Selection

The designer evaluates alternative concepts.

Evaluation criteria may include:

- Originality
- Wearability
- Feasibility
- Brand identity
- Customer needs
- Emotional impact

### Output

A selected design direction.

---

# Stage 5 — Garment Definition

The selected concept becomes more concrete.

Design decisions include:

- Garment category
- Silhouette
- Proportions
- Construction approach
- Functional requirements
- Decorative elements

### Output

A preliminary garment specification.

---

# Stage 6 — Material Selection

Suitable materials are explored.

Considerations include:

- Fabric behaviour
- Texture
- Weight
- Stretch
- Drape
- Comfort
- Sustainability
- Availability

### Output

Candidate material combinations.

---

# Stage 7 — Personalisation

The garment is adapted to the intended wearer.

Factors include:

- Body morphology
- Measurements
- Preferences
- Lifestyle
- Accessibility
- Budget
- Cultural requirements

### Output

A personalised design proposal.

---

# Stage 8 — Technical Development

The design becomes technically defined.

Activities include:

- Pattern development
- Construction planning
- Seam placement
- Structural support
- Manufacturing considerations

### Output

Production-ready design information.

---

# Stage 9 — Evaluation

The designer reflects on the design.

Questions include:

- Does the garment express the original intent?
- Are the materials appropriate?
- Does the silhouette achieve the desired effect?
- Is the design manufacturable?
- Does it satisfy the customer's expectations?

### Output

Decision to refine or proceed.

---

# Stage 10 — Iteration

Design rarely ends after one evaluation.

Possible revisions include:

- Fabric changes
- Construction changes
- Colour changes
- Decoration adjustments
- Fit improvements
- Simplification
- New creative directions

The workflow may return to any previous stage.

---

# Design Workflow Characteristics

The workflow possesses several important characteristics.

## Iterative

Design continuously evolves through feedback.

---

## Non-linear

Designers frequently revisit earlier decisions.

---

## Multi-disciplinary

Creative, technical, cultural, commercial, and personal factors interact throughout the process.

---

## Context-dependent

Different projects may emphasise different stages.

---

## Human-centred

Creative judgement remains the responsibility of the designer.

---

# Opportunities for AI Assistance

Rather than replacing designers, AI may assist during each stage.

| Stage | Potential AI Support |
|--------|----------------------|
| Inspiration | Reference discovery, trend analysis |
| Design Intent | Intent clarification, knowledge retrieval |
| Concept Exploration | Idea generation, visual alternatives |
| Concept Selection | Explainable comparisons |
| Garment Definition | Design consistency checking |
| Material Selection | Fabric recommendations, behaviour prediction |
| Personalisation | Fit adaptation, preference modelling |
| Technical Development | Pattern assistance, manufacturability analysis |
| Evaluation | Explainable feedback, consistency analysis |
| Iteration | Alternative design suggestions |

---

# Relationship to Design DNA

Design DNA is not created in a single step.

Instead, it evolves continuously throughout the design workflow.

Each stage contributes additional knowledge to the computational representation.

As new decisions are made, Design DNA becomes progressively richer and more complete.

---

# Research Questions

This workflow raises several important research questions.

- Which stages are best suited for AI assistance?
- Which decisions require human judgement?
- How should AI preserve creative intent during iteration?
- How should uncertainty be represented throughout the workflow?
- How can feedback continuously update Design DNA?

---

# Summary

This workflow provides a conceptual model of how fashion design progresses from inspiration to a realised garment.

It serves as a bridge between human creative reasoning and computational representation.

Future versions will refine individual stages, identify decision points, and define the information exchanged between the workflow and Design DNA.
